# Full-corpus future-centred SIPC coding protocol

## Unit and scope

The article is the corpus unit; the articulated educational future is the interpretive unit. Coding concerns a projected or normatively desired arrangement of education involving AI. A current example can embody that arrangement when the source connects it to a desired educational role, purpose, relationship or direction. Mentioning AI, reporting adoption, reporting performance or discussing an unrelated technological future does not by itself establish an educational future.

Preserve the link between the future role, its evaluation and justification, the speaker, affected actors, proposed action and assigned responsibility. Do not assemble a future from unrelated claims in one article. The dominant account and explicitly distinct secondary account remain distinguishable. Applying SIPC to attributed future accounts is already analysis of imaginaries in public communication. Categories describe individual fields; marginal frequencies alone do not explain the relations composing a vision. Further relational interpretation develops this analysis; an additional named typology is optional.

## Source adequacy and eligibility

Assess source adequacy separately from future presence. A tag page, unrelated teaser, missing body or absent video transcript is a source limitation, not evidence that the published article contained no future. Preserve the original text and extraction. Recovery can replace the retained text for the same article ID only, with a retrieval ledger and a fresh extraction. Do not silently expand the corpus or substitute another publication.

Clearly articulated futures form the primary analytic corpus. Weak cases receive explicit review: retain an educational direction supported by source evidence, or mark it borderline if the relation remains implicit. Report a yes-only sensitivity analysis separately. Adequate sources without educational futures remain documented negative cases. Unresolved source defects remain a separate group and do not enter substantive absence denominators.

## Categories and application

Construct the codebook after extraction of all original IDs. Examine all twelve raw fields, including negative and borderline cases as boundaries. Each category needs a definition, inclusion and exclusion rules, anchored examples and a distinction from neighbouring categories. Do not use the earlier thematic coding or named imaginaries as starting labels. Permit multiple categories within a field when the text supports them; preserve the relation of each to the relevant future account.

Apply the frozen codebook to every eligible article through semantic reading of its extraction and, where needed, its full source. Literal keyword matching is insufficient. Missing specifications receive a missing-value code rather than an inferred actor, place, timeline or responsibility. An author's byline alone is not an articulated voice. Institutional affiliation alone does not specify the geographic reach of a future. Speakers, beneficiaries and responsible actors are separate fields.

Record amendments and recode affected records if category definitions change. Preserve a coding rationale or evidence reference sufficient to inspect contested assignments. Automated schema and quote checks establish structural consistency only; do not describe them as independent substantive agreement.

### v1.1 parsimony and evaluation rules

Role families identify the educational relation being changed; they do not encode an assumed positive or negative evaluation. The active codebook consolidates capability/pathway formation, intellectual agency, teaching work and academic judgment. Preserve their mechanisms and directions in account-level details, rather than creating a category for every variation. Access includes widened opportunity, exclusion and inequality. These opposing directions must remain visible in reconstruction and comparison.

Each attributed account requires its future statement, speaker reference, role mechanisms/directions, evaluation target, conditions and source anchor. Article-level codes summarise the primary account only; distinct secondary accounts require separate linked details. Do not flatten all voices into the primary account. Positive, negative, mixed and unsettled evaluation concern the specific target, not AI in general. A protective ban can be a desired arrangement, while its prevented harm is a negatively evaluated alternative. Refusal and conditional endorsement are stance details, not automatic polarity codes. Mixed evaluation requires one speaker weighing benefits and harms of the same future; rival speakers disagreeing does not suffice.

Do not impose equal numbers of positive and negative categories or examples. Examine contrary cases and keep explicit absences and source limitations. No claim about the corpus favouring positive futures is warranted before coding and denominator checks. Explicitly open prediction is not automatically withheld value judgment; no stated evaluation is MISSING.

## Reconstruction and comparison

Reconstruct candidate futures by examining how educational purposes, technological roles, authority, actor relations, justifications and prescriptions connect within accounts and recur across publications. Support each reconstruction with linked source evidence and inspect counterexamples and internal tensions. Accounts sharing an AI application can articulate different futures; accounts using different applications can share an educational ordering.

Use the existing outlet grouping as an exploratory comparison of institutional settings, with its classification documented. Compare constituent relations, voices, certainty, justification and prescriptions as well as prevalence. Inspect variation within groups and domination by individual outlets. Report article-level denominators, source exclusions and borderline sensitivity. Group differences do not establish that ownership or political control caused a particular future, nor that one group is outside state supervision.
