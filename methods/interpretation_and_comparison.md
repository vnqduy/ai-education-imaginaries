# Reconstructing educational futures from SIPC records

Applying SIPC to source-linked future accounts is already analysis of imaginaries in public communication. The interpretive work below develops that analysis; it is not a prerequisite for calling all previous work imaginary analysis. A separate named typology is optional. Constituent frequencies alone do not explain the relations composing a future vision; repeated references to a technology or educational topic are insufficient.

Methodological basis: Brause et al. (2025), https://doi.org/10.1177/13548565251338192, use framework-guided thematic analysis. Distinguish the analytical object (sociotechnical futures) from the coding technique (which may use content or thematic analysis).

For each candidate account, specify the desired or anticipated educational order: what education is for, what AI does, how the learner/teacher/institution is positioned, who can define or govern change, why this order is justified and how it should be realised. Demonstrate the connections within sources. Do not join one speaker's desired future to another speaker's warning or action as though they formed a shared account.

Trace recurrent relations across articles and inspect sources supporting alternative explanations. Distinguish recurrence of one press release or syndicated narrative from articulation by different actors. A shared application may sustain competing purposes; a shared purpose may be realised through different applications. Preserve ambivalence, conditions and explicitly resisted futures instead of reducing every article to optimism/pessimism.

For each provisional reconstruction report:

- the relational claim and its distinction from a technology topic;
- source-anchored constituent relations and actor attributions;
- supporting articles and variation between them;
- counterexamples, internal tensions and competing arrangements;
- prescriptions, responsibility and whether enactment is described, proposed or absent;
- outlet and temporal distribution with eligible denominators;
- limitations on calling the account collective, stabilised or institutionally performed.

Use descriptive frequencies as context. Article counts measure coverage in this corpus, not public acceptance, causal political influence or the strength of collective commitment. Interpret outlet-group differences alongside within-group variation, producing-publication uncertainty, concentration in individual outlets and source/borderline sensitivity. Specify the evidence for recurrence and contestation in public communication. Do not infer institutional stabilisation, public acceptance or material enactment solely from news recurrence; their absence does not invalidate analysis within the communicative scope of SIPC.
