# Phong cách tác giả — áp dụng cho toàn bài (21 October 2026)

Các nguyên tắc dưới đây được rút từ giọng viết của bản Introduction do tác giả chốt. Áp dụng cho mọi phần khi soạn hoặc chỉnh sửa; nhiệm vụ và cách dùng bằng chứng vẫn tuân theo chức năng riêng của từng phần. Khi hướng dẫn phong cách cũ mâu thuẫn với các thống nhất này, ưu tiên mục này.

- **Cập nhật 22 October 2026:** Không dùng ngôi I trong lời tác giả; dùng “this study” cho lập luận và “the author” khi cần xác định trách nhiệm nghiên cứu. Giữ ngôi thứ nhất trong trích dẫn nguồn. Dùng nhất quán tiếng Anh–Anh, gồm problematisation, judgement, personalisation và programme; giữ nguyên tên tài liệu gốc, citation keys và mã định danh. Quy định này thay thế các hướng dẫn bên dưới cho phép dùng I.
- **Giữ giọng học thuật, trực diện, có lập trường và thiên về giải thích.** Sức nặng đến từ lập luận, bằng chứng và lựa chọn từ chính xác. Giữ sự nghi ngờ, chất vấn của tác giả ở những chỗ nó thực hiện công việc phân tích; không làm phẳng thành văn trung tính, vô danh.
- **Mở đoạn bằng điểm thực chất cần xác lập.** Không dẫn nhập dài, thông báo tầm quan trọng hoặc dùng câu chuyển chung chung để trì hoãn ý chính. Trong Methods, điểm thực chất có thể là quyết định thiết kế hoặc thao tác cần giải thích.
- **Nối lập luận từng bước.** Câu sau phát triển câu trước; đoạn sau tiếp nhận câu hỏi còn mở hoặc thêm một phân biệt cần thiết. Nhận định, giải thích, dẫn chứng và suy luận cần nối thành mạch, không thành các mảnh độc lập. Không áp một công thức cứng cho mọi đoạn.
- **Giữ các bước suy luận khi rút gọn.** Cắt ý lặp và câu thừa trước khi cắt mắt xích giải thích. Một độ dài phù hợp không có nghĩa là chuyển nhanh qua nhiều ý và bắt người đọc tự nối chúng.
- **Giữ nhịp câu tự nhiên.** Dùng câu vừa đến dài khi cần trình bày quan hệ giữa các ý, xen câu ngắn ở điểm chốt hoặc chuyển trọng tâm. Tránh chuỗi câu ngắn rời rạc và nhịp văn đều đều; không chồng nhiều danh từ trừu tượng vào một câu.
- **Thể hiện lập trường bằng lựa chọn từ vừa đủ.** Không thêm động từ phô trương, khẩu hiệu hoặc tính từ cường điệu để làm câu có vẻ sắc sảo. Dùng chủ động khi cần làm rõ ai lập luận, quyết định hoặc hành động.
- **Dẫn nghiên cứu qua phát hiện và công việc lập luận của nó.** Nêu tác giả thấy gì, trong bối cảnh nào và phát hiện đó phát triển nhận định đang viết ra sao. Tránh chuỗi tên tác giả hoặc những tóm tắt hoán đổi được cho nhau như “có khác biệt”, “có nhiều quan điểm”. Không kể lại cùng nghiên cứu ở nhiều phần nếu không thêm công việc phân tích.
- **Giải thích khái niệm qua chức năng phân tích.** Cho thấy khái niệm giúp xem xét quan hệ hoặc phân biệt nào. Không dùng lý thuyết để trang trí câu hoặc kéo dài lịch sử khái niệm khi không cần cho câu hỏi nghiên cứu.
- **Khẳng định rõ, đúng mức bằng chứng.** Ưu tiên các động từ như shows, examines, traces, identifies. Dùng may khi thực sự đặt một khả năng cần nghiên cứu; không rải lời dè chừng hoặc câu cảnh báo sau mỗi nhận định. Phân biệt nguồn nói gì, tác giả diễn giải gì và kết quả nghiên cứu cho thấy gì.
- **Không dùng sóng đôi tu từ để tạo vẻ sắc sảo.** Tránh “not merely X, but Y”, “not X but Y” và các câu đối xứng kiểu “X makes Y problematic, while Y legitimises X”. Viết rõ từng bước của cơ chế. Không biến mọi khác biệt thành hai cực hoặc lặp ý bằng những từ ngày càng trừu tượng.
- **Dùng “this study” khi nêu mục tiêu và cách tiếp cận trong Introduction và Literature Review.** Các phần khác chọn chủ thể phù hợp với nhiệm vụ: nguồn/người phát ngôn cho tuyên bố được quy thuộc; I cho hành động nghiên cứu hoặc diễn giải của tác giả khi cần. Không thay trách nhiệm cá nhân thực tế bằng cách nói vô chủ chỉ để đồng nhất hình thức.
- **Giữ đúng chức năng của từng phần.** Introduction và Literature Review đặt vấn đề, trình bày nghiên cứu trước và chuẩn bị cách phân tích; không nói trước kết quả của chính nghiên cứu, gán sẵn khác biệt giữa nhóm báo hoặc đưa chi tiết hình thành mẫu như số bài/số báo lên trước Methods. Methods trình bày thiết kế và thao tác; Results trình bày bằng chứng và diễn giải sát nguồn; Discussion phát triển ý nghĩa; Conclusion chốt đóng góp. Kết quả của nghiên cứu trước vẫn được dùng để dựng lập luận. Abstract được tóm tắt kết quả theo chức năng riêng.
- **Chỉnh ít nhất cần thiết để người đọc hiểu đúng ý tác giả.** Sửa ngữ pháp, chỗ mơ hồ, thiếu bước nối và lặp ý; giữ cách đặt vấn đề, nhịp suy nghĩ và từ ngữ có chủ đích. Không tự ý viết lại thành giọng trơn tru hơn nhưng mất chất riêng. Độ dài không tự nó là lý do để mở rộng hoặc rút gọn.

# Author’s writing preferences

Hãy áp dụng phong cách sau khi viết và chỉnh sửa bài nghiên cứu của tôi:
Giọng văn
- Chân phương, sắc sảo, tự tin. Nói thẳng phát hiện và dùng bằng chứng để làm rõ.
- Không rón rén, dè chừng hoặc tự làm yếu câu bằng những lời rào đón lặp lại. Giới hạn phương pháp đặt ở Methods/Limitations, không thêm một câu cảnh báo sau mỗi kết quả.
- Tự tin nhưng chính xác: phân biệt điều dữ liệu cho thấy với diễn giải của tác giả. Không dùng khái niệm mạnh hơn bằng chứng.
- Dùng từ cụ thể, câu tự nhiên và các đoạn có mạch lập luận. Tránh thuật ngữ phô trương, khẩu hiệu, câu chung chung và nhịp văn đều đều.
- Tránh lặp từ  và kiểu viết nhiều câu ngắn, rời rạc. Mỗi câu phải phát triển ý của câu trước.
- Không nói trước result ở các đoạn trước result
Results
- Tổ chức quanh vài finding rõ ràng.
- Mở đoạn bằng phát hiện; tiếp theo là những con số hoặc trích dẫn cần thiết; kết thúc bằng diễn giải ngắn sát bằng chứng.
- Không đọc lại toàn bộ bảng trong văn bản. Bảng giữ chi tiết; phần chữ làm nổi bật tương phản, quan hệ hoặc mẫu hình đáng chú ý.
- Kết hợp số liệu với đọc sát. Số liệu cho biết mức độ phổ biến; đọc sát cho biết nội dung và cách các yếu tố liên hệ với nhau.
- Không ép mọi đoạn phải có số hoặc trích dẫn. Chọn bằng chứng phù hợp với nhận định.
- Giữ các finding cũ có sức giải thích khi bổ sung luận điểm mới. Rút những đoạn lặp hoặc liệt kê ít thông tin, không thay toàn bộ câu chuyện theo mỗi ý mới.
Discussion
- Đặt luận điểm trung tâm ở vị trí dẫn dắt, rồi giải thích các finding đóng góp vào luận điểm đó thế nào.
- Phát triển ý nghĩa và đối thoại với văn liệu; không kể lại Results bằng từ khác.
- Có thể đưa ra diễn giải về cơ chế hoặc quá trình, nhưng phải chỉ rõ bước nối từ bằng chứng tới diễn giải.
- Không biến mọi khác biệt thành đối lập. Các phương án có thể bổ trợ nhau, cùng tồn tại hoặc khác nhau về cách thực hiện.
- Abstract, Introduction, Discussion và Conclusion phải thống nhất về luận điểm chính.
Cách làm việc
- Ưu tiên chọn lọc và làm rõ bằng chứng hiện có trước khi mở rộng phân tích.
- Không thêm kiểm định, mã hóa mới hoặc hình/bảng chỉ để bài trông phức tạp hơn.
- Khi góp ý, nêu ngắn gọn: chỗ nào chưa đạt, vì sao, và sửa cụ thể ra sao.
- Không tự bịa thông tin phương pháp, độ tin cậy hoặc tài liệu tham khảo còn thiếu.

# Academic Writing Style Guide

## 1. Writing identity

The writing style is **analytical, evidence-led, conceptually precise, and restrained**. It aims to make complex arguments understandable without simplifying their intellectual substance.

The writing should sound like a researcher who knows exactly what the evidence can establish, what remains uncertain, and why the distinction matters.

It should not sound like someone trying to demonstrate sophistication through elaborate vocabulary, excessive theorisation, or rhetorical emphasis.

The central principle is:

**Make the argument intellectually strong through the quality of reasoning, not through the complexity of the language.**

The preferred voice is calm, confident, critical, and direct. It should be accessible to readers outside the immediate theoretical tradition while retaining the precision expected in academic research.

---

## 2. Core writing principles

### 2.1. Clarity before sophistication

Use straightforward academic English.

Prefer familiar, precise words over abstract or unnecessarily elaborate alternatives.

For example:

**Avoid:**
> The discursive constitution of technologically mediated educational futurities illuminates the complex interrelationship between sociotechnical imaginaries and institutional configurations.

**Prefer:**
> These accounts show how expectations about AI shape what educational institutions are expected to change.

The second version is preferable because it identifies the relationship being examined without making the reader decode the sentence.

Complex concepts are acceptable when they perform necessary analytical work. Complexity for its own sake is not.

### 2.2. Every sentence must contribute

Each sentence should perform at least one identifiable function: establish a finding, introduce evidence, explain a relationship, clarify a distinction, develop an argument, or qualify a conclusion.

Remove sentences that merely announce importance without explaining it.

**Avoid:**
> This finding highlights the complex and multifaceted nature of AI imaginaries in contemporary educational discourse.

**Prefer:**
> The same educational future could be justified through different problems and proposed responses.

The preferred sentence tells the reader what was found.

Avoid repeating a finding in increasingly abstract language. Once a point has been established, move the argument forward.

### 2.3. Show the relationship, not just the categories

Analytical writing should explain how concepts, observations, and processes relate to one another.

Do not stop at identifying themes.

**Descriptive:**
> The articles discussed AI literacy, teacher preparation, and national development.

**Analytical:**
> Teacher preparation and AI literacy were often linked to national development, making educational change part of a wider ambition to build technological capacity.

The second version explains what connects the categories and why that connection matters.

The preferred progression is:

**Observation → Relationship → Interpretation → Implication.**

Not every paragraph needs all four components, but the overall argument should develop through them.

### 2.4. Make distinctions explicit

Much of the intellectual value of the writing should come from distinguishing phenomena that might otherwise appear similar.

Important distinctions include:

- A future's visibility versus its influence.
- An actor being discussed versus being given a speaking position.
- A desirable future versus a desirable implementation.
- Criticism of an AI application versus rejection of AI adoption.
- A rationale for intervention versus the problem representation underlying it.
- A proposed outcome versus evidence that the outcome occurred.
- Patterns across a corpus versus interpretations of selected accounts.

Explain these distinctions when they change how evidence should be understood. Do not repeatedly restate them as generic caveats.

---

## 3. Argumentation

### 3.1. Lead with the claim

Begin analytical paragraphs with the point the paragraph will establish.

Avoid lengthy introductory sentences that delay the argument.

**Avoid:**
> In order to develop a more comprehensive understanding of the ways in which educational futures are represented in Vietnamese news media, it is necessary to consider the distribution of different future roles.

**Prefer:**
> Educational AI futures received unequal attention across Vietnamese news coverage.

The evidence and interpretation should follow naturally.

### 3.2. Build arguments progressively

Each paragraph should answer a question raised by the previous paragraph or add a necessary distinction.

A strong sequence might be:

1. Establish that two futures dominate coverage.
2. Explain how their educational purposes differ.
3. Show that less prevalent futures can support the dominant ones.
4. Identify what this distribution reveals about the structure of public expectations.

Avoid treating paragraphs as independent observations connected only by phrases such as *Furthermore*, *Moreover*, or *Additionally*.

Use transitions that express the actual logical relationship:

- *By contrast,* when evidence differs.
- *However,* when a qualification changes the interpretation.
- *This distinction matters because…* when explaining analytical significance.
- *The same account also…* when showing a connection.
- *Rather than…* when identifying an alternative interpretation.

Do not use transitional phrases mechanically.

### 3.3. Prefer analytical tension over rhetorical opposition

Avoid constructing artificial binaries.

For example, public/central and commercial/general outlets should not be presented as opposing ideological camps unless the evidence demonstrates such a division.

Instead, explain the particular dimensions on which they differ and those on which they overlap.

**Prefer:**
> Public/central outlets gave greater attention to institutional preparation, while both outlet groups covered learning support and educational judgment at similar rates.

This is more informative than claiming that one group supported AI and the other questioned it.

### 3.4. Do not force every finding into the theory

Theory should help explain relationships in the evidence.

It should not determine what the evidence must mean.

If a finding is descriptive, present it descriptively. If the evidence supports a more substantial conceptual interpretation, develop that interpretation explicitly.

Avoid inserting theoretical terminology into every paragraph merely to maintain an academic tone.

---

## 4. Evidence and interpretation

### 4.1. Evidence comes before theoretical claims

The writing should establish what the material shows before discussing its broader implications.

In Results, begin with findings rather than theoretical assertions.

**Avoid:**
> Sociotechnical imaginaries are inherently political constructions that privilege particular forms of educational knowledge.

**Prefer:**
> Learners were represented as affected by educational change in 97.6% of articles but appeared as speakers in only 12.1%.

The second sentence provides an empirical basis for later discussion of whose perspectives become publicly visible.

Theoretical interpretation should build from such observations rather than replace them.

### 4.2. Use quotations for analytical work

A quotation should reveal something that a numerical result or paraphrase cannot convey as effectively.

Introduce the quotation by identifying the relevant speaker, setting, or claim.

After the quotation, explain what its wording or reasoning reveals.

Do not simply restate it.

A useful structure is:

**Finding → Context → Quotation → Interpretation.**

For example, a provider's account of AI-supported teaching may show how automation is justified as a way to preserve teachers' human responsibilities.

The interpretation should explain that relationship rather than repeat the provider's promises.

Do not include quotations merely to give every category an example.

### 4.3. Keep claims proportionate to evidence

Use precise language to distinguish different levels of support.

When analysing news accounts:

- *The article presents…* describes a representation.
- *The speaker argues…* attributes a claim.
- *The account connects…* identifies a relationship within the text.
- *The comparison shows…* describes an empirical pattern.
- *This suggests…* introduces a qualified interpretation.

Avoid moving from *an article presents* to *society believes* without evidence supporting that transition.

Similarly, do not treat media visibility as proof of institutional adoption, policy influence, or public acceptance.

### 4.4. Qualify precisely, not defensively

Academic caution should make the argument more accurate, not weaker.

**Avoid:**
> Although the findings may potentially suggest certain possible differences, further research would be needed before any conclusions could be drawn.

**Prefer:**
> The groups differed in the visibility of institutional preparation, but the comparison does not establish differences in policy influence.

The second version states both the finding and its limit.

Do not append generic limitations to every paragraph. Qualify claims where the distinction affects their meaning.

---

## 5. Conceptual writing

### 5.1. Define concepts through their analytical function

Introduce theoretical concepts by explaining what they allow the study to examine.

For example:

**Sociotechnical imaginaries** help examine how anticipated technological arrangements are connected to desirable collective futures.

**Public communication** provides a setting in which these futures are articulated, evaluated, attributed to speakers, and made visible.

**Problematization** helps examine how proposed changes are justified through representations of what requires intervention.

Definitions should prepare the reader for the analysis.

Avoid extensive conceptual histories unless they are necessary to establish the research problem.

### 5.2. Combine theories through a specific analytical problem

Do not combine frameworks merely because they are conceptually compatible.

Explain what each framework can establish and what remains difficult to see without the other.

For this research, the central relationship is:

**Whose future receives public visibility, and what educational problem makes that future appear necessary or desirable?**

The analysis of public imaginaries identifies anticipated arrangements, evaluations, speakers, rationales, and proposed actions.

Problematization examines how those rationales and actions construct the conditions that supposedly require change.

Their combination is useful because the visibility of a future and the justification for pursuing it are related but analytically distinct questions.

### 5.3. Avoid overstating conceptual contributions

Do not claim to have developed a new theory when the study has applied existing approaches in a productive combination.

A contribution can be substantial without being revolutionary.

Prefer formulations such as:

> The analysis extends research on educational imaginaries by examining how anticipated futures are connected to representations of educational problems.

Avoid:

> This study fundamentally reconceptualises the relationship between technological imaginaries and educational governance.

Unless the evidence and argument genuinely support such a claim.

## 6. Section-specific writing rules

### Introduction

The Introduction should establish a research problem, explain why existing scholarship does not fully address it, and identify what the study examines.

Its progression should be:

**Context → Intellectual problem → Research gap → Analytical approach → Research questions → Contribution.**

Do not preview detailed findings.

Do not describe the paper's significance through generic claims about the rapid growth of AI or the transformative potential of technology.

Explain why the particular research question matters.

### Literature Review

The Literature Review should construct the intellectual problem, not catalogue studies.

Organise it around analytical questions and disagreements.

Each subsection should clarify what existing research helps explain, what remains unresolved, and why the present study requires a particular approach.

Avoid ending every paragraph with *more research is needed*.

Do not introduce empirical findings from the present study to demonstrate the literature gap.

### Methods

Methods should be transparent, chronological where appropriate, and specific.

Explain what was done, how decisions were made, and what the procedures allow the study to claim.

Distinguish sampling units, coding units, analytical units, and interpretive cases.

Report AI-assisted procedures accurately. Do not invent human validation, intercoder agreement, model settings, or reproducibility claims.

Avoid unnecessary theoretical discussion once the analytical approach has been established.

### Results

Results should be organised around findings, not simply around coding categories or tables.

Each subsection should have a clear empirical argument.

Use statistics to establish patterns, source passages to clarify their meaning, and interpretation to explain relationships.

Do not repeat all values from a table in prose.

Do not make a subsection a sequence of quotations followed by paraphrases.

Keep descriptive findings and qualitative interpretations distinguishable.

### Discussion

Discussion should explain what the findings contribute to existing scholarship.

It should not reproduce Results in more theoretical language.

A strong Discussion should answer:

**What can we now understand about educational AI futures that was difficult to see before this analysis?**

Develop a small number of substantive arguments rather than assigning one paragraph to every finding.

Discuss implications and limits without claiming policy effects, institutionalisation, or social influence that were not examined.

### Conclusion

The Conclusion should return to the research problem and state the study's principal contribution.

It should be concise.

Do not introduce new evidence, repeat every finding, or end with generic statements about the importance of future research.

---

## 7. Sentence-level preferences

### Sentence structure

Prefer sentences of moderate length, usually with one central claim and a clearly expressed relationship.

Longer sentences are appropriate when qualifications are necessary, but avoid stacking several abstract nouns and subordinate clauses.

Vary sentence length naturally.

### Voice

Prefer active voice when the actor matters.

> The researcher reviewed the source passages.

Use passive voice when the procedure or result matters more than the actor.

> Article-level codes were aggregated for descriptive comparison.

Do not force active voice into every sentence.

### Vocabulary

Prefer:

- *shows* over *demonstrates unequivocally*
- *examines* over *interrogates*, unless interrogation is conceptually necessary
- *connects* over *establishes a nexus between*
- *differences* over *divergences*, unless the distinction matters
- *proposed changes* over *transformative interventions*
- *anticipated futures* over *futurities*
- *appeared more frequently* over *was significantly more prominent*, unless statistical significance was tested

Use technical terminology consistently when it identifies an established analytical concept.

### Avoid generic academic phrases

Minimise phrases such as:

- *It is important to note that…*
- *In today's rapidly evolving technological landscape…*
- *This highlights the complex and multifaceted nature of…*
- *This finding underscores the importance of…*
- *It is worth mentioning that…*
- *A nuanced understanding of…*
- *The intricate interplay between…*
- *This raises important questions about…*

These phrases are not inherently incorrect, but they often delay or substitute for substantive analysis.

State the actual point instead.

---

## 8. Paragraph construction

A strong analytical paragraph generally contains four elements:

**Claim.** What does the paragraph establish?

**Evidence.** What observations, figures, quotations, or sources support it?

**Reasoning.** How does the evidence support the claim?

**Development.** What distinction or implication moves the argument forward?

For example:

> Public/central outlets gave greater attention to institutional preparation than commercial/general outlets. AI Literacy appeared in 58.3% of public/central articles, compared with 47.5% of commercial/general articles, while National Development appeared in 25.7% and 13.1%, respectively. These differences were accompanied by more frequent references to public authorities, curriculum changes, and resource provision. The contrast therefore concerned not only which futures received attention but also the institutional responsibilities associated with them.

This paragraph works because each sentence advances the argument.

The first establishes the finding. The second supplies evidence. The third adds a related pattern. The fourth explains the relationship.

Not every paragraph needs this exact structure. The important requirement is logical progression.

---

## 9. Critical reasoning

The writing should be critical without being adversarial.

Criticism should emerge from identifying assumptions, tensions, exclusions, alternative explanations, or limits of evidence.

Do not equate critical analysis with negative evaluation.

For example, a ministry's proposed AI strategy can be examined critically without claiming that its goals are misguided.

The analytical question is not simply whether the strategy is desirable.

It is how anticipated AI expansion establishes institutional readiness as a problem, what responsibilities follow from that formulation, and which questions remain outside the proposal.

Similarly, an educational technology provider's account should not be dismissed merely because it promotes a commercial platform.

Its institutional position matters, but the analysis should examine the specific relationship between its promised future, representation of educational needs, and proposed intervention.

**Critique should explain how an argument works before evaluating its limitations.**

---

## 10. Tone and intellectual stance

The preferred tone combines five qualities.

**Confident:** State supported findings directly.

**Restrained:** Do not exaggerate their significance.

**Curious:** Remain attentive to unexpected relationships and tensions.

**Fair:** Represent speakers' arguments accurately, including those the researcher may question.

**Precise:** Distinguish what the source says, what the analysis finds, and what the researcher infers.

Avoid moralising about technological adoption.

Avoid treating technological progress as inevitable unless describing a source's own representation of inevitability.

Avoid assuming that official accounts are inherently uncritical or commercial accounts inherently promotional.

Avoid interpreting every omission as deliberate exclusion.

The purpose is to understand how educational futures are publicly constructed and justified, not to classify actors as progressive, conservative, optimistic, or resistant.

## 11. Common problems to eliminate during revision

### Redundancy

Do not repeat a finding in the introduction, body, and conclusion of the same subsection without adding analytical value.

### Over-explanation

Do not explain a straightforward distinction in three paragraphs when one will suffice.

### Theoretical decoration

Remove theoretical language that does not change the interpretation.

### Evidence dumping

Do not present multiple statistics or quotations without explaining the relationship among them.

### Unsupported inference

Do not turn an observed association into a causal claim.

### Artificial symmetry

Do not force every future role, outlet group, or case to receive equal space. Allocate space according to analytical importance.

### Premature interpretation

Do not announce empirical findings in the Introduction or Literature Review.

### Repetition between Results and Discussion

Results establish and interpret the evidence. Discussion explains what the resulting insights contribute to scholarship.

### Excessive caution

Do not weaken clear findings with unnecessary hedging.

### Inflated conclusions

Do not end sections by declaring broad transformations in education, governance, or society that the study has not demonstrated.

## 12. Preferred editorial process

When revising academic writing, follow this sequence.

**First, identify the argument.** Determine the single most important point of the section and the evidence supporting it.

**Second, inspect the evidence.** Check figures, quotations, source attribution, coding definitions, denominators, and methodological limits before rewriting.

**Third, reconstruct the progression.** Organise paragraphs so that each adds evidence, develops a relationship, or introduces a necessary distinction.

**Fourth, rewrite for clarity.** Remove redundant sentences, generic academic language, unnecessary abstractions, and forced transitions.

**Fifth, check analytical discipline.** Ensure that every interpretation follows from the evidence and does not exceed the study's design.

**Sixth, check the section's function.** Remove material that belongs in another section.

**Finally, edit at sentence level.** Improve rhythm, terminology, concision, and readability without changing the substance of the argument.

Do not treat editing as a purely stylistic exercise. Strong academic editing may require restructuring paragraphs, clarifying conceptual distinctions, or removing claims that the evidence does not support.

However, **never silently change findings, methodological procedures, source meanings, or analytical categories to make the writing more elegant**.

---

## 13. Non-negotiable rules

1. **No invented evidence.** Never fabricate citations, quotations, statistics, methods, validation procedures, or source details.
2. **No inflated claims.** Match every conclusion to the level of evidence.
3. **No theoretical name-dropping.** Concepts must perform identifiable analytical work.
4. **No generic filler.** Every sentence must advance the argument.
5. **No unnecessary repetition.** A repeated idea must add a new distinction or implication.
6. **No artificial binaries.** Preserve overlap, ambiguity, and differences within categories.
7. **No premature findings.** Introduction and Literature Review establish the problem; Results present the evidence.
8. **No Results disguised as Discussion.** Discussion develops contributions rather than retelling findings.
9. **No automatic negativity.** Critical analysis is not synonymous with criticism of AI.
10. **No complexity for prestige.** Prefer the clearest formulation that preserves the full analytical meaning.

---

## 14. Final writing standard

The ideal manuscript should read as though every paragraph has been written to answer a specific intellectual question.

The reader should be able to follow the argument without repeatedly returning to earlier sentences, decoding unnecessary terminology, or guessing how the evidence supports the interpretation.

The writing should make the researcher's reasoning visible: what was observed, how different observations relate, what those relationships mean, and where the evidence stops.

It should be theoretically informed without being theory-heavy, critical without being dismissive, and accessible without sacrificing analytical depth.

**The ultimate standard is simple: write with enough precision that the argument does not need rhetorical emphasis to appear important.**
